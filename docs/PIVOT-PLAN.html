Market Twin · pivot plan · Nebius × NVIDIA hackathon · 14 Oct 2026

Bring your business. See which plan survives today's news.

Same engine, same Nemotron models, same deadline. What changes is who the app is for: a person with a real decision to make instead of a judge watching a recording.

Today "Can an AI CEO beat a textbook pricing rule?" A recorded race in a made-up GPU market. When it ends, the visitor has nothing to do.
→
After the pivot "A tariff just hit my business. Which plan should I pick, and how bad could it get?" The visitor types in their own business, and Nemotron tests plans against real news, the way an analyst would.
The user's journey

Five steps, about two minutes

1Describe your businessTwo sentences, or pick one of the 3 example businesses.Super
2See the news that hits youHeadlines about your product and shipping route, each with what it would cost you.Tavily Super
3Watch the AI analyst workIt proposes plans, tests each one in the simulator, and improves on them.Ultra Lightning
4Compare plans × shocksOne grid: which plan wins, and which one is still OK if things get worse.Engine · no LLM
5Take the decision memoOne page: the plan, the profit range, the risks, and the news quotes behind it.Ultra

Click any cell in the grid and the existing pixel arena and charts replay that plan week by week. Waves 1–2 stay in the app as the "watch it play out" view.

3 · The AI analyst at work

  • Nemotron Ultra decides which plans to test. The engine computes every number it reads. LLMs still never make up outcomes.
  • 3 rounds, about 2–3 plans each, then a final check across all shocks.
  • The shopper groups react to each plan's price. This is where Lightning's 12 groups earn their place.
  • This is the "agent" in Best Apps and Agents: it plans, uses a tool, reads the result and revises.

4 · Plans × shocks

  • This is your "run multiple scenarios" idea. Rows are plans (the AI's plus the user's own), columns are how bad the shock gets.
  • Each cell shows the average result plus the bad case, because a small business cares most about not going broke.
  • The comparison is against "do nothing", not the cost-plus rule. That's a fairer baseline and easier to understand.
  • The user can add their own plan row with 4 sliders: price change, extra stock, when, and supplier.
  • Click a cell to replay it (next screen).
Engine · NumPy 24 cells × 1,000 runs takes about a second locally. The Nebius Serverless Job runs 10,000 per cell for the "verified" badge. The job finally has a real job to do.

Scope

What stays, what's new, what's cut

Stays (waves 1–2)

  • The whole engine: market, stock, price game, stats, Monte Carlo
  • Router, rate limiter, telemetry, spend cap
  • Tavily scout and Super's shock reader
  • Arena and charts, now the "replay one plan" view
  • REPLAY mode, Nebius Endpoint + Job

New

  • Business profile schema + Super reader + 3 example businesses
  • "Plan" = 4 knobs turned into the engine's existing per-week decision schedule
  • Grid runner: plans × shocks, paired runs vs "do nothing"
  • Ultra analyst loop with a test_plan tool
  • News impact per headline
  • Decision memo
  • New screens 1–4 and 6

Cut or demoted

  • "AI vs cost-plus" as the headline (it becomes one row option)
  • 500 sprites (stay at 200)
  • χ² switching test
  • Cournot, Cobb-Douglas, Switchyard (already P2)
  • Deep-Scout auto trigger (replaced by the news screen)

Needs your OK: changes to docs/EVENTS.md (new events: agent steps, grid cells, memo) and the agent schemas. The engine contract in types.py should not need changes, because a plan becomes the Monte Carlo's existing Schedule.
